[slide] 
type=list
eyebrow=METHOD & CONSTRAINTS 
subtitle=How Was the Experiment Recorded? 
items=Weight recorded daily in a spreadsheet.
Weekly averages calculated.
Compared with weekly targets.
Morning/rested weight used as the first measurement.
Weekly trends considered more useful than individual fluctuations.
Fasting varied between approximately 1 and 4 days per week. 

scope=Blood sugar, hormones, calorie intake, controlled
exercise, sleep, stress and life events were not measured.

[slide] 
type=analysis
eyebrow=SO WHAT? 
title=Analysis & Reflection 
items=Fasting appeared effective at removing recently gained weight.\nWeight repeatedly
returned toward approximately 15 stone.\nShorter repeated fasts appeared
sufficient.\nFasting and gentle activity appeared complementary.\nFeasting
supported satiety and sustainability.\nConsistent measurement reduced
emotional disappointment from daily fluctuations.\nWeekly trends were
more informative than individual readings. 
note=Measurement + feedback + fasting + gentle exercise appeared more important than fasting alone.

closing=The weight-management problem begins to look more like a feedback system than a diet.

[slide] 
type=list
eyebrow=WHAT NEXT? 
title=What Was Learnt? 
items=
Regular measurement matters.
Weekly trends matter more than daily fluctuations.
Short repeated fasts were easier to sustain than prolonged restriction.
Weight can be viewed as a feedback-control problem.

[slide] 
type=list
title= What Is Significant? 
items=
Weight reduced from approximately 16.5 stone to approximately 15 stone.
Reduction occurred without calorie counting.
Weight increased when measurement and fasting stopped.
Measurement itself appeared to influence behaviour.

type=list
title=Questions Raised

[slide] 
type=list
eyebrow=MEASUREMENT 
title=Note on Measurement
items=
Sometimes the best weight during a day was recorded rather than a fixed time.
Spreadsheet data entry can contain human error.
A simple measurement/control system might allow food quality and quantity to be adjusted.
Future work could consider sugar, fat and glycogen stores.

note=Measurement is critical to the process.

[slide] 
type=list
eyebrow=CLOSING REFLECTION 
title=A Defined Period of Observation

items=
Meaningful weight change can occur without following a particular restricted-food diet.
Fasting can produce weight loss without focusing on food quality and quantity during eating periods.
Measurement created greater awareness of food choices.
Long-term management may depend more on measurement, feedback and small behavioural adjustments than finding the perfect diet.
Fasting was useful, but not the whole story.
The key lesson was managing weight over time realistically and sustainably.
